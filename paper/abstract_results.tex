We assess permutation scoring on shared ASR candidate banks: three frozen Whisper-small checkpoints, five languages, one main fit per selector and 30-second inputs. Global adds a permutation contrast to clean-audio confidence, length and consensus (Conf). Relative to Conf, it lowers macro word error rate (WER) by 0.1410 percentage points on FLEURS and 0.1824 on Common Voice v27 (CV27). Frozen masked-language-model scoring (Text) improves on Global by 0.9864 and 1.2043 points. Adding Global to Text changes CV27 WER by $-0.0104$ points (adjusted 98.33\% client interval [$-0.0280$, $+0.0069$]), without establishing further gain. Post-exposure controls do not establish a contrast-specific advantage; TRAIN-subset refits show sensitivity. The original Local-over-Global increment is unestablished; minimum-WER fitting likewise does not establish gains over corresponding ridge fits. Measured costs include score acquisition and Text's additional resources. Evidence supports modest gains beyond internal confidence, with further benefit beyond external text unestablished.
