Do permutation-derived ASR scores add value beyond confidence, consensus and external text? We compare matched selectors on shared candidate banks from three frozen Whisper-small checkpoints in five languages. Global adds permutation scores to clean-audio confidence, length and consensus (Conf), lowering macro WER by 0.1410/0.1824 percentage points on FLEURS/CV27. Frozen masked-language-model scoring (Text, or Conf+PLL) improves over Global by 0.9864/1.2043 points. Adding Global to Text changes CV27 WER by $-0.0104$ points (adjusted 98.33\% client interval [$-0.0280$, $+0.0069$]); its incremental reduction is smaller than without Text. Parameterization controls do not establish a contrast-specific advantage. Local-over-Global and minimum-WER-over-ridge gains remain unestablished. One main selector fit serves all checkpoints; subset refits show sensitivity. Results use 30-second inputs and include observed-test extensions. Including score acquisition and model resources supports a conditional assessment of scoring value, without establishing further benefit beyond external text.
